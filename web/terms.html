3. Player safety

Poddle is a physical game. You swing a real phone or AirPod as a paddle and, when playing with a webcam, you step from side to side. Accidents can occur: a phone may slip from your hand, a swing may strike a lamp, or your foot may catch on a rug.

Before and during play

  • Clear the space all around you, including the space behind and above you. Check your surroundings before every swing.
  • Maintain a secure grip. Phones and AirPods are not supplied with a strap, so hold your device firmly and use a wrist strap or a case with a non-slip grip if you have one. Ensure that your hands are dry.
  • You swing toward your screen. Stand well back from your laptop or television (the webcam requires you to be a couple of metres away in any event), and never play while another person is between you and the screen or seated within reach.
  • Never deliberately throw or release your phone or AirPod. The game never requires you to do so.
  • AirPods are small. If one comes loose during play, locate it before a young child or pet does.
  • Do not play with a phone that has cracked glass.
  • Remain mindful of people, pets and fragile objects, such as televisions, screens, windows, lamps, glasses and furniture. Keep other people, particularly children, out of swinging range.
  • Play on a flat, non-slip floor, wearing suitable footwear or with bare feet, with no tripping hazards nearby.
  • Stop playing if you experience pain, dizziness or discomfort, and take breaks. If you have a health condition or injury that physical activity could affect, consult a doctor before playing.
  • Do not play in any situation that requires your attention elsewhere, such as while driving, or near stairs or roads.

6. Public courts and spectators

  • Listed courts appear in the public court list, together with both players' names, the score and the number of people watching. Anyone may watch.
  • Courts created with a code are unlisted, not locked. Anyone who has the 4-character code or link may take an empty seat or watch. Share a code only with people with whom you wish to play.
  • Spectators can see your name, your score and every movement and swing you make. Spectators' names are shown to the players when they begin watching or request to play, and on any emote they send.
  • Tournaments: the host's name is listed while players are joining, and anyone with the tournament code can see every player's name and the bracket.
  • Your phone pairing QR code and code link a phone to your game. Do not display or share them (for example, on a stream), and do not scan another person's code without their permission.
  • Poddle does not record or replay matches. However, anyone watching could film their own screen, so please play with the understanding that others can see you.
