Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 24, 2026. Effective: September 24, 2026.

Poddle is a free pickleball game in your browser at poddleball.com. This page explains, in plain words, what information the game uses, why, who can see it, and how long it lasts. It also covers the Poddle Helper app for Mac. The rules for playing are in our Terms of Use.

The short version

  • No accounts, no cookies, no ads, no analytics. We never sell your information or share it for advertising.
  • Your webcam video stays on your computer. It is read inside your browser to find roughly where you are standing. No video, image or face data is sent or saved. We don't identify anyone.
  • Your position and your paddle's motion are sent live to our game server and on to your opponent and anyone watching, so the match can be played. They are not stored.
  • Your display name is public to other players and spectators. Don't use your full real name.
  • Our server holds your IP address and name in memory only, to run the game and stop abuse. We keep no database, and it is all gone when the server restarts.
  • Questions or requests: hello@danielrltan.com.

For teens and parents

  1. Pick a nickname, not your real name. Strangers can see it.
  2. Camera video never leaves your computer. Only a rough position is sent.
  3. There's no chat, no profile and no account. The game server forgets your name when the match ends or the server restarts.
  4. Anyone with a court code can watch or join an empty seat, so only share codes with friends. Don't show your phone's pairing code on a stream.
  5. Under 18? Ask a parent before playing or installing Poddle Helper.
  6. Questions, or want something deleted: hello@danielrltan.com.

2. What we use and why

Poddle has no sign-up. We don't ask for your email, age, location, phone number or payment details. Here is everything the game does use.

Device permissions the game may ask for

  • Motion and orientation, on your phone, when you tap Start on the phone page. On an iPhone this is the Motion & Orientation prompt. Motion is only read on the secure (https) page.

  • Local network (in Chrome), if you play with an AirPod. This lets the page talk to Poddle Helper on your own Mac. If you have played before and haven't chosen between phone and AirPod, or you chose AirPod, the page may quietly check whether Poddle Helper is running on your Mac, and Chrome may ask about local network access.

You can say no to any of these, or turn them off later in your browser or phone settings. Without the camera, the game moves your player for you (Auto). Without phone motion or an AirPod, you can still watch matches.

What we don't do

We don't sell personal information, share it for advertising, build profiles, or make automated decisions about you. We don't use your information to train AI. We run no ads, analytics tools, tracking pixels or social media plug-ins on Poddle.

3. Your webcam

If you play with Body movement, the game uses your computer's webcam to see where you are standing. Here is exactly what happens.

  • Your browser asks your permission first. The camera is only asked for when you take a seat to play. Spectators are never asked. The camera captures video only, with no sound.
  • The video is analysed inside your browser, on your computer, by Google's open-source MediaPipe software, which Poddle hosts itself. For each video frame it finds a face and a few points on it (eyes, nose, mouth, ears), or, if it can't see your face, points on your body such as your nose and shoulders.
  • From those points the game keeps just one smoothed centre point: roughly where your head and shoulders are in the picture. The points themselves are discarded straight away, frame by frame, and never leave your browser.
  • No video, photo, frame or list of face or body points is ever sent to our server or anyone else, and none is saved.
  • We don't recognise or identify anyone. We don't create, collect or store any face template, faceprint, or record of face geometry.
  • MediaPipe, as Google publishes it, can send Google usage statistics. We have switched that off in our copy, so it sends nothing to Google or anyone else.

What is sent: the centre point turns into your player's position on the court. That position, along with your paddle's angle, goes to our game server about 60 times a second. The server passes it on to your opponent and to anyone watching that court, so they can see you move. It is not stored.

Your choice: you don't need the webcam to play. When your browser asks, choose Don't Allow (or Block), and the game moves your player for you (Auto). Choosing Auto in Settings does not turn the camera off: once you allow it, the camera stays on until you close the Poddle tab, though its video still never leaves your computer. You can turn camera access off at any time in your browser's site settings.

4. What other players can see

Poddle is played with and in front of other people. Please choose a display name you're happy for strangers to see. Don't use your full real name, especially if you're under 18.

  • Listed courts: anyone who opens the court list sees the names of both players, the score and how many people are watching.
  • Unlisted courts (made with a code): they don't appear in the list, but anyone who has the 4-character code or link can join an empty seat or watch. A code is short, so treat it like a room number, not a password.
  • Tournaments: the host's name is listed while players sign up. Anyone with the tournament code sees all players' names and the bracket.
  • On a court: players and spectators see the players' names, scores, movements and swings. Players also see the name of each spectator who starts watching or asks to play, and everyone on the court sees the name of any spectator who sends an emote.

There is no chat. Emotes are a fixed set of 7 emoji.

7. How long we keep things

  • Display names, IP addresses, court and tournament details: held in the game server's memory while your court, tournament or anti-spam cooldown lasts, and at the latest until the server restarts. The server writes none of it to disk. The server restarts when we update the game, or when it sleeps with nobody connected. If you are mid-match during a restart, your own tab sends back your court code, name and score so the match can carry on.
  • Motion, position and swing data: passed along live and not stored.
  • Webcam video: never leaves your computer and is never saved.
  • Activity logs: the server writes short activity lines, such as "court joined" or "tournament started: 4 players", with court codes. Our logs don't include display names, IP addresses or tab IDs. Fly.io's own network and platform logs may record IP addresses and web addresses. We don't export, copy or archive any of these logs. Fly.io keeps them for about 7 days, after which they are deleted automatically.
  • Browser storage: stays on your device until you clear it, or until you close the tab for the tab ID and pairing code.
  • Emails you send us: kept until your request is resolved, then deleted within 12 months.

11. Security

We protect information by keeping as little as possible. The site and the game connection use encrypted connections (HTTPS). Game data is held in memory, not in a database. Webcam video never leaves your device. Phone motion is checked and only passed to the one tab that paired with that phone.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach of security involving personal information creates a real risk of significant harm, we'll notify the privacy regulators as the law requires (within 72 hours where the GDPR applies), including the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada. Since we have no contact details for players, we'll also post a notice on the Poddle home page. We'll keep a record of every breach.

12. Poddle Helper for Mac

Poddle Helper is an optional, free, open-source Mac app that lets an AirPod be your paddle.

  • It reads only your headphones' motion, using Apple's motion framework: which way the AirPod points, how fast it turns and moves, and which bud it is. macOS asks your permission (Motion & Fitness) first.
  • It passes that motion only to other programs on the same Mac (it never goes over the internet), and only while a page is connected to it. It accepts web pages from Poddle's own addresses (poddleball.com and its older poddle.fly.dev address), pages served from your own Mac, and local apps on your Mac.
  • It makes no internet connections of its own and collects nothing. It has no analytics. Its menu links simply open web pages in your browser.
  • Once the motion reaches Poddle in your browser, it is used like phone motion, as described above.

Its source code is on GitHub, under the MIT license.
