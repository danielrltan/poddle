Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 25, 2026. Effective: September 25, 2026.

Poddle is a free pickleball game that runs in your browser at poddleball.com. This Privacy Policy explains, in plain language, what information the game uses, why it is used, who can see it and how long it is kept. It also covers the Poddle Helper application for Mac. The rules for using Poddle are set out in our Terms of Use.

Summary

  • Signing in with Google is optional and is never required to play. We use sign-in cookies only if you choose to sign in. We do not use advertising or analytics, and we never sell your information or share it for advertising purposes.
  • Your webcam video remains on your computer. It is processed within your browser to determine approximately where you are standing. No video, images or face data are transmitted or saved. We do not identify anyone.
  • Your position and your paddle's motion are transmitted live to our game server and relayed to your opponent and anyone watching, so that the match can be played. They are not stored.
  • Your display name is publicly visible to other players and spectators. Please do not use your full real name.
  • Our server keeps game statistics in a database in Canada, linked to a random identifier in your browser or, if you sign in, to your account. Your IP address and the display name you type are held in memory only, in order to operate the game, prevent abuse and check that matches are fair, and are never written to the database.
  • Statistics are optional and private. They are shown only to you. You may turn them off, download them or delete them, and your account, at any time in section 9 of this page.
  • Questions or requests: hello@danielrltan.com.

For teens and parents

  1. Use a nickname rather than your real name. It is visible to strangers.
  2. Camera video never leaves your computer. Only an approximate position is transmitted.
  3. There is no chat. Your statistics are private and are shown only to you. The game server discards the display name you type when the match ends or the server restarts.
  4. Signing in is optional. If you sign in, your username is saved and is shown to other players whenever you play, so do not use your real name in it. Players under 18 may sign in only with a parent's or guardian's permission.
  5. Anyone with a court code can watch or take an empty seat, so please share codes only with friends. Do not display your phone's pairing code on a stream.
  6. Under 18? Please ask a parent before playing or installing Poddle Helper.
  7. For questions or deletion requests, please contact hello@danielrltan.com.

What we do not do

We do not sell personal information, share it for advertising purposes, or build advertising or marketing profiles. The only automated decision that Poddle makes is whether a match counts toward statistics, which is decided by fixed fair-play rules and affects nothing other than your own statistics. It never prevents you from playing, and no one reviews it unless you ask us to. If you believe that a result was wrongly excluded, you may contact us. We do not use your information to train artificial intelligence. We do not run advertisements, analytics tools, tracking pixels or social media plug-ins on Poddle.

3. Your webcam

If you play with Body movement, the game uses your computer's webcam to determine where you are standing. The process is described in detail below.

  • The first time you take a seat, before anything is requested, Poddle shows a screen explaining why the camera is used, and you choose Allow camera or Play without camera. Only if you choose Allow does your browser ask for permission. Camera access is requested only when you take a seat to play; spectators are never asked. The camera captures video only, without sound.
  • The video is analysed within your browser, on your computer, by Google's open-source MediaPipe software, which Poddle hosts itself. For each video frame, the software locates a face and a few points on it (eyes, nose, mouth and ears) or, if it cannot detect your face, points on your body such as your nose and shoulders.
  • From those points, the game retains only a single smoothed centre point, representing approximately where your head and shoulders are in the image. The points themselves are discarded immediately, frame by frame, and never leave your browser.
  • No video, photo, frame or list of face or body points is ever transmitted to our server or to anyone else, and none is saved.
  • We do not recognise or identify anyone. We do not create, collect or store any face template, faceprint or record of face geometry.
  • MediaPipe, as published by Google, can send usage statistics to Google. We have disabled this feature in our copy, so it sends nothing to Google or to anyone else.

What is transmitted: the centre point is converted into your player's position on the court. That position, together with your paddle's angle, is sent to our game server approximately 60 times per second. The server relays it to your opponent and to anyone watching that court, so that they can see you move. It is not stored.

Your choice: the webcam is not required to play. Choose Play without camera (or, when your browser asks, select Do not Allow or Block), and the game will move your player automatically (Auto). Play without camera is remembered on that browser, and the camera is not requested again unless you turn it on. Selecting Auto in Settings does not turn the camera off: once you have granted access, the camera remains on until you close the Poddle tab, although its video still never leaves your computer. You may disable camera access at any time in your browser's site settings.

4. Information visible to other players

Poddle is played with, and in view of, other people. Please choose a display name that you are comfortable with strangers seeing. Do not use your full real name, particularly if you are under 18.

  • Listed courts: anyone who opens the court list can see the names of both players, the score and the number of people watching.
  • Unlisted courts (created with a code): these do not appear in the list, but anyone who has the 4-character code or link can take an empty seat or watch. Because codes are short, a code should be treated like a room number, not a password.
  • Tournaments: the host's name is listed while players join. Anyone with the tournament code can see all players' names and the bracket.
  • On a court: players and spectators can see the players' names, scores, movements and swings. Players can also see the name of each spectator who begins watching or requests to play, and everyone on the court can see the name of any spectator who sends an emote.
  • Statistics: your statistics, including your record against Matt and your personal bests, are shown only to you. We do not publish leaderboards. If a match does not count because both players appear to be using the same network or the same browser, both players are told that the match did not count.
  • Usernames: if you sign in and choose a username, it replaces your display name whenever you play. It is shown with a registered-player mark to opponents and spectators, in the public court list and in tournament brackets. Because a username stays the same from match to match, other players can recognise you each time you play. Do not include your real name or any other personal information in it. Your statistics remain private.

Poddle has no chat feature. Emotes are limited to a fixed set of 7 emoji.

7. Data retention

  • Display names that you type, IP addresses, court and tournament details: held in the game server's memory for as long as your court, tournament or anti-spam cooldown lasts, and at the latest until the server restarts. The server writes none of this information to disk. A scrambled form of the network address is kept in memory for up to 24 hours for fair-play checks, as described in Information we use and why. The server restarts when we update the game, or when it goes to sleep because no one is connected. If you are in the middle of a match during a restart, your own tab sends back your court code, display name and score so that the match can continue.
  • Motion, position and swing data: relayed live and not stored.
  • Webcam video: never leaves your computer and is never saved.
  • Activity logs: the server writes brief activity entries, such as "court joined" or "tournament started: 4 players", with court codes. Our logs do not include display names, IP addresses or tab IDs. Fly.io's own network and platform logs may record IP addresses and web addresses. We do not export, copy or archive any of these logs. Fly.io retains them for approximately 7 days, after which they are deleted automatically. Log entries about matches record the court code, the side that won, the score, the kind of match (including the Matt level) and whether it counted, never who played.
  • Statistics saved without an account: deleted automatically 90 days after the last match recorded for them, or 7 days after it if only one match was ever recorded. If you clear your browser storage, you can no longer view or delete those statistics yourself, and they are deleted automatically on the same schedule.
  • Accounts: kept until you delete your account. An account that has not signed in or recorded a match for 24 months is deleted automatically, together with its statistics.
  • Sign-in sessions: deleted when you sign out, 180 days after you signed in, or when you delete your account.
  • Match records: deleted 30 days after the match, or sooner, oldest first, if our database approaches its size limit. If either player deletes their data sooner, that player's link to the record is removed at once.
  • Former usernames: after a username is changed, or an account is deleted, a simplified form of the former username, which is not linked to any account, is kept for 30 days after a change or 90 days after a deletion, so that no one else can immediately take it to impersonate the former holder.
  • Backups: our hosting provider takes a snapshot of the database storage each day, and each snapshot is kept for 5 days. Information that you delete is removed from the live database immediately and is removed from all backups within 5 days. Any copy of the database that we make for maintenance is deleted within 5 days. We do not keep any other copies of the database. Expired information is removed by an automatic clean-up that runs when the server starts and every 24 hours.
  • Browser storage: remains on your device until you clear it or, in the case of the tab ID and pairing code, until you close the tab.
  • Emails you send us: kept until your request has been resolved, and then deleted within 12 months.

11. Security

We protect information by keeping as little of it as possible. The website and the game connection use encrypted connections (HTTPS). Live game data is held in memory. Our database holds no email addresses, no IP addresses and no display names typed by guests. Device identifiers and sign-in cookies are stored only as one-way hashes, which cannot be converted back into the original values, although they remain linked to your statistics. The sign-in cookie cannot be read by the page's scripts and is sent only over encrypted connections to poddleball.com. Information that is deleted is overwritten in the database file, not merely marked as deleted. Webcam video never leaves your device. Phone motion data is validated and relayed only to the single tab that paired with that phone.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach of security involving personal information creates a real risk of significant harm, we will notify the privacy regulators as required by law (within 72 hours where the GDPR applies), including the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada. Because we hold no contact details for players, we will also post a notice on the Poddle home page. We will keep a record of every breach.

12. Poddle Helper for Mac

Poddle Helper is an optional, free and open-source Mac application that allows an AirPod to serve as your paddle.

  • It reads only the motion of your headphones, using Apple's motion framework: the direction in which the AirPod points, how quickly it rotates and moves, and which earbud it is. macOS requests your permission (Motion & Fitness) first.
  • It passes that motion data only to other programs on the same Mac (it is never transmitted over the internet), and only while a page is connected to it. It accepts connections from web pages at Poddle's own addresses (poddleball.com and its earlier poddle.fly.dev address), pages served from your own Mac, and local applications on your Mac.
  • It makes no internet connections of its own and collects no information. It contains no analytics. Its menu links simply open web pages in your browser.
  • Once the motion data reaches Poddle in your browser, it is used in the same way as phone motion, as described above.

Its source code is available on GitHub under the MIT license.
